What is the Dalston LTN?

A low traffic neighbourhood between the A10, Dalston Lane and Amhurst Road: a 24-hour bus gate on Shacklewell Lane, filters on Foulden, Farleigh, Arcola and Cecilia Roads, turn bans and two School Streets. The aim is to stop cut-through traffic on residential streets, which is a fair goal.

The problem: that traffic moves onto Dalston Lane, Amhurst Road, the A10 and Graham Road, where the Council itself describes a "traffic standstill at peak times". The Pembury Circus works have only just finished, and officers say they won't have the data to assess them until December.

What the Council's own decision report admits:

  • Its traffic model "struggles" with roads nearing full capacity, "such as Graham Road".
  • It predicts 200 to 300 more vehicles in the morning peak hour on Dalston Lane westbound towards Pembury Circus, next to the only monitor in Hackney over the legal NO2 limit in 2024.
  • None of its 23 baseline traffic counts are on Graham, Navarino or Greenwood Road, so it cannot measure traffic pushed onto them.
  • The ambulance service warned the Ridley Road ban would force crews onto Graham Road, which it said "will be taking a significant increase in traffic volume".
  • Bus journey times were checked only for routes 488 and 236, not the 38, 242 or 277 on Graham Road.
  • It proposes lifting the ban on turning right from Shacklewell Lane onto the A10, opening a new route north via Dalston Lane and Ridley Road that Graham and Navarino Roads will feed. No traffic modelling has been done.

Our ask: get the data first. Defer these orders until the impact on Hackney Central is known and mitigation is in place.
